M.S. Thesis · San Diego State University · 2025

A Multi-Criteria Analysis Approach to Assess Wildfire Risk to Populated Areas in San Diego County

Developed a GIS-based Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) framework to assess wildfire risk (WFR) across San Diego County by integrating three submodels: Wildfire Hazard (ignition density, live fuel moisture, vegetation, fire history), Wildfire Exposure (structure density, Wildland-Urban Interface classification), and a Social Vulnerability Index (demographic, socioeconomic, and environmental indicators). Relative weights were derived using the Analytical Hierarchy Process (AHP) and combined through weighted linear combination to produce spatially explicit risk scores, aggregated to 55 Populated Places and 13 Tribal Reservations.

Variance-based sensitivity and uncertainty analysis (Monte Carlo simulation, Sobol indices) evaluated model robustness. Results identified Boulevard and southeastern Tribal communities as areas of highest risk, with WUI classification and structure density as the strongest drivers of exposure.

Marshall Plan Foundation Fellowship · Carinthia, Austria · 2025

Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis for Assessment and Site Selection of Retention Basins for Flood Risk Management in Southern Austria

As a Marshall Plan Fellow hosted at Carinthia University of Applied Sciences (CUAS), built and tested a GIS-based MCDA framework to optimize site selection for decentralized flood retention basins. Flood hazard, exposure, and land-suitability criteria were integrated into three submodels and aggregated with a Weighted Linear Combination into a composite Flood Mitigation Suitability (FMS) surface.

Candidate sites were derived from the FMS surface with a binary threshold and minimum-area filter, followed by carrying-capacity estimation and rank-based prioritization. Global variance-based sensitivity and uncertainty analysis (quasi-random weight perturbation, Sobol indices) found Elevation, Land Cover Type, and Soil Type most influential on site rankings. From 753 candidate polygons, 20 priority sites were identified and ranked by storage capacity potential, rank stability, and population coverage.

Field Survey & Technical Report · Back Country Land Trust · 2020

Goldspotted Oak Borer Assessment — Long Potrero Preserve

Led fieldwork and GIS data management for a full-preserve survey of 899 coast live oak and Engelmann oak trees at Long Potrero Preserve, evaluating infestation by the goldspotted oak borer (GSOB) — a beetle responsible for killing more than 80,000 oaks across Southern California. Each tree was tagged, geolocated, and scored across three physical indicators: crown dieback, D-shaped exit-hole density, and bark staining.

Findings showed GSOB symptoms in 349 of 899 trees (38.8%) — up from 33.2% in a 2018 baseline survey. The resulting report, co-authored with Megan King and Michael Terlep, recommended a tiered treatment plan (chop-and-solarize for the most severely infested trees, systemic insecticide or barrier spray for moderate cases) to the Back Country Land Trust.
